# Code of conduct

swiftdc is a small project, and we want it to be a good place to work for
anyone who shows up, whatever their experience, background or identity, and
including people who use assistive technology.

## What we expect

- Be kind and assume good faith. Most friction is a misunderstanding.
- Critique the code, not the person. "This guesses at arity" is a review.
  "You clearly don't understand ARM64" isn't.
- Back up technical claims with something anyone can re-run. Disagreeing is
  fine, and a reproducible command settles most disagreements.
- Welcome newcomers. A first pull request that needs work gets help, not
  dismissal.
- Respect accessibility. Write text that works with a screen reader: no
  information that exists only in a screenshot, color or image.
- Respect people's time and their no. If someone declines a change, a review
  or a conversation, let it go.

## What isn't acceptable

- Harassment, insults, slurs, or demeaning jokes about anyone's identity,
  ability or background.
- Sexual language or imagery, or unwelcome attention of any kind.
- Threats, or encouraging anyone to harm themselves or others.
- Publishing someone's private information without their permission.
- Sustained disruption of discussions, issues or reviews.
- Using swiftdc's spaces to share binaries or code you have no right to
  share, or to plan attacks on systems you aren't authorized to test.

## Where this applies

Everywhere the project lives: issues, pull requests, reviews, commit
messages, discussions, and anywhere else you're representing swiftdc.

## Reporting

If something here is broken, or you aren't sure whether it is, email
camden@bopp.net or camden.bopp@icloud.com. Reports are read by the
maintainer, Camden Bopp, and kept private. You'll get a reply, and you don't
need proof or a perfect account to send one.

If the problem involves the maintainer, you can also use
[GitHub's abuse reporting](https://docs.github.com/en/communities/maintaining-your-safety-on-github/reporting-abuse-or-spam).

## What happens next

Depending on what happened, the response may be a private word, a request
to edit or remove something, a temporary ban from the project's spaces, or a
permanent one. The aim is to stop the harm, not to punish. Whoever reported
it will be told what was done, as far as privacy allows.
